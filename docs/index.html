The world above

Every city is an island of green.

Edravoss rises in walls of stone. Its people live where the mountains relent: enclosed basins of farmland, water and crowded settlement.

Magic gathers at elevation. On the highest ridges it turns weather violent and distance treacherous. The passes can be crossed, but never cheaply and never with certainty.

So food, letters, tools, strangers and armies travel below. A city may never see its neighbour across the peaks, yet depend upon it for survival.

What the walls remember

The tunnels are road, refuge and archive.

Those who return leave word for those who follow.

Most marks are hurried: an arrow cut beside a failing lamp, a flood line daubed in charcoal, three strokes where five travellers once entered. Only changes expected to outlast a season earn deep carving from a trained hand.

Stone is durable, not eternal. Water softens shallow cuts. Repairs take old accounts with them. Collapses bury whole arguments about what happened and who was to blame.

The walls do not preserve one history. They preserve the argument.

Road Speech

Words made for darkness.

Settlements separated for generations no longer speak alike. Below ground, travellers still understand the same blunt necessities.

Road Speech is short enough to shout through water and echoes. Its written form—the Cut—reduces those phrases to rough, durable marks. It records what happened, rarely why.

WATER RISE. Leave the lower way.

FIVE ENTER. THREE RETURN. Count the living.

The Broken Road

The first journey

One of the main food roads has collapsed. The settlement cannot wait for it to be cleared.

Several teams are sent into the tunnels to find another way. This story follows one small party as they leave home, cross into a neighbouring settlement and descend beyond the roads people still remember.

The collapse was deliberate. Nobody yet knows by whom.
