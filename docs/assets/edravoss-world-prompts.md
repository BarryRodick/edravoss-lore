# Edravoss world artwork prompts

These are the original prompts retained for `edravoss-world.jpg` before its
world-life revision. Each edit used the preceding generated image as Image 1.
The subsequent edit is recorded in [world-life-prompts.md](world-life-prompts.md).

## 1. Base Below scene

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: preview portrait hero illustration for a dark fantasy lore website
Primary request: Show the Below of Edravoss as a vast underground country rather than a network of tunnels.

Scene:
A full-frame view from inside an immense natural underground vault. There is no surface landscape, no cutaway and no split panel. The unseen ceiling disappears into blackness far above. The chamber is so large that layered cliffs, high shelves and distant rock ridges create their own dark horizon. A cold subterranean river winds across a broad basin floor through silt banks, gravel bars and shallow flooded ground.

Subject and trade:
A small working pack caravan crosses firm high ground in the lower middle distance. Eight to twelve sturdy ordinary pack animals carry matched waterproof panniers, wrapped bundles and small casks. Hauliers walk beside them in practical worn clothing. The caravan is compact but not crowded, and tiny against the vault. It is following a short surviving fragment of an old weathered road which fades into a rough natural track rather than continuing across the image.

Two scouts are well ahead, testing possible crossings with staffs and lamps. One lower pass has flooded. Another opening is sealed by an old collapse. A higher irregular route appears usable today and bends out of sight between rock shoulders. The caravan travels on stable ground; no rocks are falling around it. One lone worn waystone stands on a dependable shelf with no readable marks. Far away, a few warm lamps on another high shelf suggest a modest waystation, not a town.

Visual logic:
The viewer should understand that the vast basins are stable enough for caravans, while floods and collapses continually change the short passes between them. The Below must feel like an entire hostile country with its own geography, not infrastructure directly beneath a settlement.

Style and composition:
Dark painterly editorial fantasy realism with natural varied stone texture, atmospheric depth and restrained detail. Portrait 2:3 book-cover composition. High wide viewpoint. The cavern and river dominate; people and animals provide scale. Charcoal, blue-black, wet slate, muted brown and sparse weathered-gold lamplight. Quiet, dangerous and practical rather than spectacular.

Avoid:
No conventional tube-shaped tunnels, repeated arches, sculpted walls, masonry vaults, mine workings, continuous paved road, wagon train, carts, sledges, stairs, bridges, rails, cranes, large underground settlement, giant crystals, lava, glowing mushrooms, magical glow, monsters, active rockfall, rubble spectacle, honeycomb or repeating polygon rock texture, visible surface world, labels, text, logo or watermark.
```

## 2. Wide Above and Below composition

```text
Use case: compositing
Asset type: preview wide hero illustration for a dark fantasy lore website
Input images: Image 1 is the approved visual and subject reference for the Below on the right side. Reframe its world into a wide composition rather than simply stretching or cropping it.

Primary request:
Create one large cinematic 16:9 landscape showing two distinct Edravoss realms side by side. The left side shows the world above. The right side shows the vast Below established in Image 1. Both halves must feel like complete places with their own horizon and scale.

Left side, above ground:
An immense cold mountain basin seen from a high wide viewpoint. One isolated settlement sits small in the middle distance among irregular fields, terraces, water and rough pasture. The settlement must feel inhabited but vulnerable, not a grand capital. Sheer black ridges and snow-streaked peaks close the basin on every side. Violent charged storm clouds gather over the heights, making surface travel beyond the basin look implausible. No second nearby settlement. No visible road crossing the mountains. Natural grey daylight, wind and rain haze. The mountains and weather dominate the people.

Right side, the Below:
Preserve the physical idea, realism and mood of Image 1. A vast underground vault with cliffs, high shelves, a dark internal horizon, a winding cold river, silt banks and flood-reworked ground. A tiny working pack caravan of ordinary pack animals and hauliers follows firm high ground. Scouts test a changing crossing ahead. A lone worn waystone stands on stable ground. A few distant warm lamps indicate a modest waystation on a high shelf. No carts or sledges. The Below must feel like a whole country rather than a tunnel.

Relationship and composition:
The left occupies about 45 percent of the canvas and the right about 55 percent. Use a narrow, immense near-black mountain buttress at the centre as a natural vertical composition break. On the left it reads as the enclosing ridge of the surface basin. On the right it becomes the dark near wall of the underground vault. It separates the scenes without forming a graphic bar or rectangular panel border.
The two worlds do not physically connect within the image. There is no tunnel, shaft, staircase, road or cutaway line between them. The left has an outdoor sky and distant surface horizon. The right has a cavern ceiling lost in darkness and its own interior horizon.
Keep cold grey-blue light above and deeper blue-black shadow below, with sparse weathered-gold lamps on the right. Use one coherent painterly editorial fantasy realism across the whole image.

Framing:
Wide 16:9 panoramic composition, large high-resolution website hero, strong detail at desktop size, full bleed, balanced visual weight. Leave some dark negative space in the upper centre for flexible page cropping, but include no text.

Avoid:
No split-screen gutter, black rectangular divider, collage, diptych frame, transparent geological cross-section, town directly over a tunnel, visible route connecting the two worlds, similar adjacent towns, sculpted tunnel network, regular arches, masonry vaults, mine workings, continuous paved underground road, giant crystals, lava, glowing mushrooms, monsters, active rockfall, honeycomb or repeating polygon rock texture, labels, lettering, logo or watermark.
```

## 3. Clearer physical separation

```text
Use case: precise image edit.
Asset: wide cinematic fantasy-world explanatory illustration, 16:9 landscape.
Input: Image 1 is the edit target.

Primary goal: preserve the successful atmosphere, left surface settlement, and right-hand river caravan, but make it immediately and physically unmistakable that the left is ABOVE GROUND and the right is the BELOW, deep under the mountains. It must not read as two neighbouring valleys.

Composition and geology:
- Left 43 percent: an open-air high mountain basin seen at broad regional scale. Keep green fields, branching streams, and one small clustered town.
- Put the harsh storm sky HIGH ABOVE and BEHIND the mountain peaks. Do not let dark cloud shadow crush or visually sit directly on the green basin. Give the basin restrained cold daylight and enough atmospheric clarity to read as an inhabited surface landscape.
- Centre: show a convincing natural cut face through the mountain, with a clearly readable thickness of solid stratified rock separating the worlds. The surface ground line and mountain slopes terminate at this rock section.
- Right 52 percent: the Below at a closer human/trade-route scale, with its floor dramatically lower than the surface basin. A massive continuous rock ceiling must span the entire upper-right half, visibly resting beneath a great thickness of mountain rock. Show the rough underside, hanging stone, fissures, mineral stains and occasional water drips.
- Absolutely no sky, clouds, outdoor horizon, daylight opening, or mountain silhouette inside the right-hand world.
- The centre cut face, thick overhead rock, and vertical drop must make the spatial relationship legible in one glance: open sky above the left; buried cavern below the mountain on the right.
- Keep the cold underground river, rough bank trail, practical pack-animal caravan, walkers/scouts, a worn waystone, and a small distant shelf waystation with sparse amber lamps.
- Preserve scale contrast: the surface settlement is tiny within a wide basin; individual caravan figures and pack animals are readable in the Below.
- Do not show a neat tunnel connecting the surface town to the cavern. Movement through the Below follows shifting natural shelves, gravel bars and riverbanks, not a sculpted road.

Lighting and colour:
- Left: pale cold daylight on green-brown basin, with harsh weather concentrated over the high ridges.
- Centre: near-black rock cross-section.
- Right: enclosed blue-grey darkness, reflected river light, tiny warm lamps only.
- Strong separation but one coherent geological world.

Style: naturalistic, cinematic, high-detail matte painting with believable geology and trade logistics. Wide hero image. No graphic divider, split-screen border, labels, arrows, text, title, logo, watermark, giant crystals, lava, fantasy city, masonry tunnel, paved road, rails, bridges, or symmetrical composition.
```

## 4. Diagonal separation

```text
Use case: precise-object-edit
Asset type: preview wide hero illustration
Input images: Image 1 is the edit target and the style, world, and content anchor.

Primary request: recompose the separation between the two worlds as a strong rising diagonal from the exact BOTTOM-LEFT edge to the exact TOP-RIGHT edge.

Layout:
- The ABOVE occupies the upper-left side of that diagonal.
- The BELOW occupies the lower-right side of that diagonal.
- The dividing diagonal is a thick, exposed natural geological cut through the mountain. It should be slightly irregular and layered like real fractured strata, but its bottom-left to top-right direction must be instantly readable.
- Remove the present vertical central cliff and horizontal cave split. Replace them with this single dominant diagonal geological section.
- Treat the view as a cinematic natural-history cutaway through one mountain region, not two panels and not two adjacent valleys.

Above, upper-left:
- Preserve the broad mountain basin, branching streams, muted green fields, and one tiny clustered town.
- Keep the harsh storm front high above and behind the peaks. The green basin remains visible in cold open daylight.
- The Above is seen at a broad regional scale, with the settlement very small.
- Keep all sky and outdoor atmosphere on this side of the diagonal only.

Below, lower-right:
- Preserve the cold underground river, rough shifting riverbank route, practical pack-animal caravan, walkers and scouts, worn waystone, and small distant shelf waystation with sparse amber lamps.
- The Below is seen at a closer human scale. People and pack animals are readable.
- The rough underside of the diagonal rock mass forms the cavern roof. The cavern opens downward and rightward beneath it.
- The Below is fully buried. No sky, clouds, daylight opening, mountain silhouette, or outdoor horizon appears below the diagonal.
- Make the caravan route believable: gravel bars, broken natural shelves and riverbanks. No sculpted tunnel or permanent smooth highway.

Critical invariants:
- Preserve the restrained blue-grey and earth palette, naturalistic cinematic realism, believable geology, settlement, river and caravan.
- Make the diagonal separation much clearer than the current vertical split.
- The two regions must remain parts of one coherent geological world.
- Do not draw a graphic divider, border, label, arrow or text. The exposed rock strata itself creates the diagonal.
- No direct tunnel from the surface town, paved road, masonry, rails, bridges, giant crystals, lava, fantasy city, logo or watermark.
- Wide 16:9 composition.
```

## 5. Clear sky over the basin pockets

```text
Use case: lighting-weather
Asset type: preview wide hero illustration
Input images: Image 1 is the edit target.

Primary request: change only the weather and daylight over the ABOVE, in the upper-left region. Make the sky a little clearer specifically above the green inhabited basin pockets, so they read as isolated habitable lands held between severe mountain ranges.

Sky and light:
- Break the solid storm deck into several restrained openings of pale cold blue and soft silver-grey sky over the low green basins.
- Let calm daylight fall locally into those basin pockets, revealing their grass, streams and tiny settlement.
- Keep heavy, harsh cloud gathered around the high peaks, mountain walls and distant passes.
- The clear areas must be local weather windows, not one broad cheerful sky. Dark ridges and banks of cloud should still separate one basin from the next.
- Keep the mood remote, cold and difficult, but no longer uniformly storm-dark.
- Avoid golden sunlight, sunset colour, tropical blue, fluffy summer clouds or an idyllic pastoral mood.

Critical invariants:
- Preserve the exact bottom-left to top-right diagonal geological divide.
- Preserve all mountain shapes, the green basin geography, streams, tiny town and scale.
- Preserve the entire BELOW unchanged: cavern roof, rock, river, caravan, route, waystone, lamps, darkness and closer human scale.
- No sky or daylight enters the Below.
- Add no buildings, roads, tunnels, bridges, labels, text, logo or watermark.
- Keep the same naturalistic cinematic rendering, wide 16:9 framing and restrained blue-grey earth palette.
```
